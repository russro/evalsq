# eval2 slide outline (first pass)

Talk: October 10, 2026. Figures come from `uv run evalsq --grid 25` (`figures/`). Diagram mockups come from `uv run python slides/mockups.py` (`slides/mock_*.png`) and are used as final figures in the deck.

1. **Title.** eval2: evaluating evals before they dictate deployment decisions. Russell Ro, October 10, 2026.
2. **What is an eval? What is a benchmark?** Eval: measuring how a system does on a task with one or more metrics, per example, per task, or whole system in deployment [1][2]. Benchmark: a fixed dataset + metric, shared to compare methods [3]. Every benchmark is an eval, most evals are not benchmarks. Either way it is a proxy for $, and it only counts while it keeps predicting $ [4][5].
3. **Pipeline.** Data → Models → Eval → Deploy → $. The $ arrives L days late, which is why we decide on the eval at all. Diagram: `mock_pipeline.png`.
4. **Drift.** Three ways the world moves under a fixed eval [10]. Anchor on the joint P(x,y), split two ways: P(x,y) = P(y|x)·P(x) = P(x|y)·P(y). Each drift is one factor moving while its partner holds. Data (covariate) drift: P(x) moves, P(y|x) holds (SPY: volatility jumps in 2020, 2022) [6][7][10]. Concept drift: P(y|x) moves, P(x) holds, the same inputs now mean something else (SPY: a signal that predicted up days stops working) [8][10]. Label drift: P(y) moves, P(x|y) holds, so by Bayes P(y|x) shifts too (SPY: share of up days differs in bull vs bear years, so accuracy moves even if the model doesn't) [9][10]. Notes only, toy label-drift numbers: P(signal|up)=0.7, P(signal|down)=0.4; bull P(up)=0.6 gives P(up|signal)≈0.72, bear P(up)=0.45 gives ≈0.59. The eval sits on top of all three, and the $ only shows up L days later. No figure, three small panels in draw.io. Refs: [6] Quiñonero-Candela et al. 2009, Dataset Shift in ML (MIT Press). [7] Moreno-Torres et al. 2012, A unifying view on dataset shift, Pattern Recognition 45(1). [8] Gama et al. 2014, A survey on concept drift adaptation, ACM CSUR 46(4). [9] Lipton, Wang, Smola 2018, Detecting and correcting for label shift with black box predictors, ICML. [10] Huyen 2022, Data Distribution Shifts and Monitoring, huyenchip.com/2022/02/07/data-distribution-shifts-and-monitoring.html.
5. **Worked example: SPY trading, but worse.** Setup: SPY daily, 2010–2025. Target is next-day up/down. Features: 1/5/20-day returns, MA10/MA50 gaps, 20-day vol, day/month. Model zoo (8): LogReg, LogReg-mom, RF, ExtraTrees-vol, GBM, GBM-mom, kNN, MLP (each gets its own feature subset). Monthly loop: (1) at month t, retrain each model and score it on data through t−L (L = 21 trading days); (2) the eval metric picks one model; (3) deploy it for the month; (4) the P&L only shows up L days later. Figures: `fig1_setup.png`, `mock_timeline.png`.
6. **Evaluating the eval.** (final text) Because of the L-day lag (for both observing data and making actions), we can't just deploy the model that made money in short time frames. We have to pick on a proxy metric (or combination of metrics). Treat each metric as a selection rule then run through the monthly loop. Grade each rule by the $ it earned from 2018 to 2025. Metrics: Accuracy, AUC, Negative log loss, Bear accuracy, Lagged P&L, Combined. Figure: `mock_bench_of_bench.png`.
7. **Three heuristics to consider.** (final text) H1 Validity: does the score track $? H2 Selector stability: does the best eval stay the best? H3 Meta-model: is the score mostly measuring the regime instead of the model? Diagram: `mock_heuristics_map.png`.
8. **H1a. Does the score track $?** Accuracy correlates 0.82 with monthly Sharpe, AUC 0.35. AUC for LogReg falls to −0.55 in 2023. Figure: `fig2_h1_validity.png`.
9. **H1b. What each metric earns.** $100k deployed by each rule, net of 1bp per trade and 0.5%/yr short borrow: bear-day accuracy $320k, accuracy $209k, AUC $182k, log-loss $167k. Never switching made $344k and SPY made $287k. The metric with the best correlation does not make the most money. Fees cost the switching rules $10k to $15k (60+ swaps, 1 to 3 flips a month); at 10bp only bear-day accuracy ($220k) and never switching ($288k) are still close to SPY. Figures: `fig5_deploy.png`, `fig6_picks.png`.
10. **H2. Does the best eval stay the best?** Same monthly P&L as H1b, cut into blocked time folds (1-year and 2-year, never shuffled, so no fold sees a later regime). In each fold, rank the six rules by $. Bear-day accuracy wins 5 of 8 years and 2 of 4 two-year blocks, and never drops below 4th. But the winner rarely repeats: last fold's winner wins the next one 2 of 7 times (29%, chance 17%) at 1y, 1 of 3 at 2y. 2022 is won by AUC, the worst rule overall. Spoken line: "One split picks an eval. The next split usually picks a different one." Figure: `fig3_h2_folds.png`.
10b. **H2. What trusting the winner costs.** Regret = return points lost per fold vs the best rule in hindsight. Following last fold's winner loses 7.2 pts/yr (15.7 per 2y), better than a random rule (10.9 / 22.4) but well behind always using bear-day accuracy (3.1 / 4.7). Combined is the worst fixed rule (14.4 / 32.3). Point: a robust eval is the one that is never far from best, not the one that wins most often. Caveats: 4 to 8 folds is a stability check, not a significance test, and picking bear-day accuracy as "the fixed rule" is itself hindsight. Figure: `fig3b_h2_regret.png`. Old 2-model H2 (winner flips 3 times, RF wins 2022) moved to notes; its yearly table still feeds H3.
11. **H3. Is the score just the regime?** Predict RF yearly accuracy from that year's volatility and trend. LOO R² = −0.38, so the score still says something about the model. Figure: `fig4_h3_meta.png`.
12. **Bonus. The more you compare, the more the leaderboard lies.** Best of k GBM configs: the eval promises 6.2pt more than it delivers at k=25, 0.1pt at k=1. Eval climbs 54% → 61% as k grows, deployed stays near 54% for every k. Why: within a month, eval acc vs deployed acc correlates 0.02, so the k=25 winner is just the luckiest config on the 63-day window and falls back to the average. The configs are nearly tied anyway (52 to 56% deployed), and the month matters more than the model (spread 9.3pt across months vs 5.4pt between models in a month). Month to month the gap swings −25 to +30pt and the best k flips between 1 and 25 with no pattern. Figures: `fig7_winners_curse.png`, `fig8_gap_dist.png`, `fig9_opt_k.png`.
12b. **Learned eval. Can a model pick the model?** Ridge on the six eval signals, demeaned across the zoo, predicting next-month return minus the zoo average. No model identity, so it learns which signals matter. Trained on 2012 to 2017 months too. Refit monthly on all history $177k, on the last 36 months $191k, frozen after 36 months $149k, all net. None beat accuracy ($209k), bear-day accuracy ($320k) or never switching ($344k). Rank IC about 0 (leave one model out 0.04, t 0.9), the same story as the 0.02 within-month corr. The all-history weights settle on accuracy minus uptrend accuracy, which is close to bear-day accuracy. The frozen fit leans on lagged P&L, which flips sign after 2023: drift kills the static selector. Figure: `fig10_learned_weights.png`.
12c. **Where learned evals do work: EmbedLLM.** Task: hundreds of LLMs by thousands of benchmark questions, scored 0/1 correct. Learn an embedding per model and per question, then predict whether model M gets question Q right. Useful for (1) routing, sending each query to the cheapest model likely to get it right, and (2) estimating a new model's scores from a small subset instead of running the full suite. It works there because questions come from a fixed pool (exchangeable), a released model does not change, and the matrix is big. Stocks break all three: tomorrow is not like yesterday, models retrain, and we have 96 rows. Takeaway: a learned eval only transfers while new items look like the old ones. No figure.
13. **Insights. What predicts a good eval, and what doesn't.** Predictive: plain accuracy (corr 0.82 with Sharpe, and it stays above 0.62 in every rolling window); scoring on the regime you fear (bear-day accuracy earns the most of any rule, $320k net); grading the eval across many time folds instead of one split (H2: last fold's best eval repeats only 29% of the time). Not predictive: number of candidates k (more k inflates the bench, deployed stays flat); AUC (0.34, goes negative in 2023); combining every metric into one score (0.75 corr, $167k net, worse than accuracy alone); a learned selector under drift ($149k to $191k net, rank IC about 0); a model's bench score within a month (0.02 corr with deployed). Caveat: correlation is not money. No rule beat never switching ($344k net), and the most correlated metric was not the most profitable.
14. **What each check catches.** H1 catches concept drift (the score to $ link moves). H2 catches drift in which eval works (regime change reorders the selectors, not just the models). H3 flags a eval that measures the environment more than the model. The winner's curse is selection bias, which shows up even with no drift. A learned eval works only if items are exchangeable.
15. **Many more, depends on the task.** Coverage audits, label consistency, contamination. Discussion: what drift are you most exposed to, and would your eval notice?
